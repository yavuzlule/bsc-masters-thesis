Of the shorter poems, besides the greeting to Pippin on his return from the campaign against the Avars (796), an epistle to David (i.e., Charlemagne) incidentally reveals a delightful picture of the poet living with his children in a house surrounded by pleasant gardens near the emperor's palace. The reference to Bertha, however, is distant and respectful, her name occurring merely on the list of princesses to whom he sends his salutation. The poem De conversione Saxonum has been attributed to Angilbert. Angilbert's poems were published by Ernst Dümmler in the Monumenta Germaniae Historica.
